PhD Researcher · University of Groningen · Metamaterials

Geometry is only half the story.

Metamaterials are usually designed as if their behaviour comes from structure alone. My research shows how the material they are made of — and the conditions they work in — shape what they can do. I combine additive manufacturing, material characterisation, computational modelling and experimental mechanics to design metamaterials that are predictable, programmable and responsive.

01 · Research

How material and structure work together.

My PhD began with a simple question: why do 3D-printed polymer metamaterials so often behave differently from their designs? Answering it led me from treating material properties as a limitation to using them as a design tool.

01 / PREDICT

Predictive wave engineering

Polymers are viscoelastic: they damp waves in a frequency-dependent way. I built a framework that combines measured material properties with geometry to reliably predict how elastic waves travel through 3D-printed metamaterials.

02 / PROGRAM

Snapping metamaterials

Inspired by a click pen, I developed a cascaded mechanism that snaps through and snaps back under loading in a single direction — a self-resetting, programmable mechanical response.

Looking ahead

From responsive materials to controlled motion.

My research has moved from understanding how material and geometry govern wave propagation to exploiting that interplay for programmable mechanical function. The next step is stimuli-responsive metamaterials, where the material is not only a constituent of the structure but also a source of actuation.

I am particularly interested in combining elastic instabilities, such as snapping, with chemically and thermally responsive materials to turn slow stimuli into fast, predictable motion for soft robotics and adaptive structures. My long-term goal is to lead a research group exploring how material and structural design together can unlock new metamaterial functionalities.
